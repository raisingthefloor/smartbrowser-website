What if the browser fixed the page for you?

A smart browser repairs and reshapes any web page, on your own device, to fit the way you see, read, hear, and move. Even pages whose authors did nothing for accessibility.

The pieces now exist in ordinary browsers: the page structure, the accessibility tree, user styles, and small AI models that run locally. A first proof of concept is working. This site is where the people building it, studying it, and depending on it come together.

The idea in one minute

Accessibility today asks every website to be built for the average person with each disability. After decades of guidelines, lawsuits, and good work, about 96% of home pages still have detectable failures, and even a perfectly conforming page leaves many people out, because many needs cannot be written as testable rules.

A smart browser turns the problem around. It keeps the page the author made, and when something on it does not work for the person in front of it, the browser changes that one thing: a missing label, gray-on-gray text, a paragraph written at college level, a flashing banner. It runs on the person's own computer, with a small local model, so nothing about them leaves the device. If the page already works for them, it does nothing at all.

The goal is not a new browser. It is for this capability to be built into every common, free browser, so it is simply there wherever anyone sits down.

Who this site is for

People who make browsers

You already have the DOM, the accessibility tree, user styles, and on-device models. We want your help now to advance the idea and the code, and later to build it in. What we are asking of browser makers.

People who build, test, and study accessibility

Engineers, researchers, standards people, and people with disabilities who will judge whether this works. Nothing replaces current practice until you say it works as well or better. Ways to contribute.

Anyone working on related things

Reader modes, simplification, personalization, agents that operate interfaces, research on individual interfaces. If it could advance smart browsers, it belongs in the Library, even if accessibility was never the goal.

Smart Browser is an open collaboration to prove that a browser can fit any web page to the person using it. Everyone who joins the effort is part of it. This site is sponsored by Raising the Floor.
