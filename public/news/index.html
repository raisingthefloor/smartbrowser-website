News

What has happened, newest first. Also available as an RSS feed.

  • November 2026

    smartbrowser.site opens

    This site goes live as the shared reference point for smart-browser work: what the idea is, why it matters, where the proof of concept stands, a library of related work, and the open questions. If you know of work that belongs in the Library, send it.

  • October 2026

    TPAC 2026 breakout: the browser as a universal interpreter

    Mike Paciello and Gregg Vanderheiden presented the idea and the proof of concept at the W3C's annual technical plenary, with Steve Faulkner moderating. Chrome engineers, the BBC, Cisco, and W3C staff responded with interest and with hard questions about scale, security, responsibility, analytics, testability, and energy. Two suggested next steps: a community group, and remembering that even one criterion lifted off authors is worth doing. Summary · The open questions · Session proposal.

  • October 2026

    First-draft proof of concept shown at M-Enabling 2026

    In one person-month, a first-draft smart browser running entirely on a five-year-old laptop made pages that fail WCAG satisfy about half of the Level A and AA criteria, and showed 85 settings a person can ask for. Details and caveats.

Smart Browser is an open collaboration to prove that a browser can fit any web page to the person using it. Everyone who joins the effort is part of it. This site is sponsored by Raising the Floor.

The proof of concept was funded in part by the National Science Foundation (Grant 2312370) and the National Institute on Disability, Independent Living, and Rehabilitation Research, Administration for Community Living, U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (Grant 90REGE008). Opinions here are those of the authors, not the funding agencies.
