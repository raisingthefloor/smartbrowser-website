Transit service notice: the failures

What is wrong with the transit notice demo page, and which WCAG 2.2 success criteria each problem fails. This list is the page's test specification.

Deliberate failures in the transit notice demo page
ProblemWCAG 2.2 criteriaWhat a smart browser might do
Body text is 11 pixels in light gray (#9a9a9a) on white, about 2.8:11.4.3 Contrast (Minimum); 1.4.4 Resize Text in spiritRaise contrast to the person's minimum; enforce a minimum text size
Text is fully justified1.4.8 Visual Presentation (AAA)Set left alignment
Two icon buttons have no accessible name4.1.2 Name, Role, Value; 2.4.4 Link Purpose for the equivalent linksInfer names from the icons and add them as labels
“ALERT” blinks indefinitely2.2.2 Pause, Stop, HideStop the blinking and note that it was blinking
The detour map (inline SVG) has no text alternative1.1.1 Non-text ContentDescribe the image; answer questions about it
No heading markup; the title is a styled span1.3.1 Info and Relationships; 2.4.6 Headings and LabelsMark the title as a heading
No page language declared beyond the root; no landmarks1.3.1 Info and Relationships (landmarks are a technique, not a requirement)Add landmarks for navigation and main content
“Click here” link2.4.4 Link Purpose (In Context) is arguably met by the sentence; 2.4.9 Link Purpose (Link Only) (AAA) failsRewrite the link text to say where it goes
Email field has no label, only a placeholder1.3.1; 3.3.2 Labels or Instructions; 4.1.2Add a visible label tied to the field
Error shown as the word “Invalid” in red, not associated with the field, with no instruction3.3.1 Error Identification; 3.3.3 Error Suggestion; 1.4.1 Use of ColorAssociate the message with the field, add an icon and the word Error, and say how to fix it
Paragraph written at college reading level; idiom “back on track”; abbreviations ADA and ETA unexpanded3.1.5 Reading Level (AAA); 3.1.3 Unusual Words (AAA); 3.1.4 Abbreviations (AAA)Rewrite to the person's chosen level; make the idiom and abbreviations explainable

Smart Browser is an open collaboration to prove that a browser can fit any web page to the person using it. Everyone who joins the effort is part of it. This site is sponsored by Raising the Floor.

The proof of concept was funded in part by the National Science Foundation (Grant 2312370) and the National Institute on Disability, Independent Living, and Rehabilitation Research, Administration for Community Living, U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (Grant 90REGE008).
